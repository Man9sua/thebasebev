A **hojicha latte** is roasted Japanese green tea powder whisked with a little hot water and topped with steamed or cold milk. It tastes nutty, toasty and close to caramel, with almost none of the grassy bitterness of matcha. For a café, it is a fast build: 3 g of pure hojicha powder for a hot 8 oz cup, 4 g for an iced 12 oz cup.

For operators, the appeal is practical:

- **One powder, many drinks.** The same powder works hot, iced, in foam and as a dusting.
- **Evening sales.** Guests who avoid a strong coffee or matcha after sunset often accept a roasted tea latte.
- **Low waste.** A pure powder keeps longer than brewed tea concentrate and is dosed per cup.

## What is the difference between hojicha and matcha?

Both come from the tea plant. Matcha is ground from shaded, unroasted leaves and tastes grassy and umami. Hojicha is roasted at high heat, which turns the leaves brown and gives a softer, toasty profile with less astringency.

Hojicha is usually lower in caffeine than matcha, but it is not caffeine free. One 2024 Japanese analysis found [26 mg of caffeine in a serving of roasted green tea versus 28 mg in standard green tea](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/lifestyle/food/1004523/hojicha-vs-matcha-what-s-the-difference/story/), and a powdered hojicha drink can contain more than a lightly steeped cup. On the menu, "lower caffeine" is accurate; "caffeine free" is not.

## How much hojicha powder per latte?

These are the doses from our Barista Standard for pure powders (the same card covers matcha, ube and butterfly pea):

**Hot hojicha latte, 8 oz cup**

1. Add **3 g** hojicha powder to a bowl or pitcher.
2. Add **40 ml** hot water at **70°C** and whisk until there are no lumps.
3. Steam **170 ml** milk and pour over the tea.

**Iced hojicha latte, 12 oz cup**

1. Whisk **4 g** hojicha powder with **40 ml** water at **70°C**.
2. Fill the cup with **80 g** ice and add **210 ml** cold milk.
3. Pour the hojicha over the milk for a layered look, or shake everything together.

Leave **10 to 20 ml** of headroom in every cup so the drink travels without spills.

At these doses, 100 g of powder gives about **33 hot** or **25 iced** lattes, which makes ordering and par levels easy to plan.

## How do you make a hojicha cream latte with cold foam?

The iced version sells better with a foam crown. Use our [cold foam topping](/topping) standard: **50 g** topping powder to **250 ml** milk, a **1:5 ratio**, whipped with a hand frother, an electric hand mixer or an aerating blender.

1. Build the iced hojicha latte above, reducing the milk slightly to keep 10 ml of headroom.
2. Crown with **50 to 60 g** of cold foam.
3. Finish with **1 to 2 g** of dusting or freeze dried garnish.

A vanilla, condensed milk or cream cheese foam works with the roasted profile. Keep to our flavour rule: a maximum of **two flavour accents** per drink, so the tea stays recognisable.

## Which flavours pair with hojicha?

Roasted notes sit well next to caramel, banana, vanilla and dates. In our own signature builds, the lab pairs hojicha with salted caramel and banana caramel [RAF bases](/raf-coffee), which add creaminess and medium sweetness without coffee. Plant milk works too, so the drink fits dairy free orders.

Avoid strong acids: they fight the roasted flavour and can split the milk.

## FAQ

### Is a hojicha latte sweet?

Hojicha itself tastes roasted rather than sweet, so the sweetness comes from what you add: the milk, a medium sweet base or a sweet cold foam.

### Can hojicha be served with plant milk?

Yes. Oat and other plant milks work for both hot and iced versions.

### Does hojicha need special equipment?

No. A bowl or pitcher, a whisk or hand frother, a scale for the 3 g or 4 g dose and a steam wand for the hot version are enough.

### Can hojicha be served at night?

It is lower in caffeine than matcha but still contains some, so describe it as lower caffeine rather than caffeine free.
