A custom drink base is a dry premix formulated for one chain: its flavour, its sweetness level and its dose, so every outlet makes the same signature drink from one weighed powder. Development usually runs in five stages: a written brief, a prototype built on existing bases, lab samples of the custom base, a pilot in one or two outlets, and a full rollout with a recipe card and training.

## Why do coffee chains develop their own drink base?

A signature drink is only a signature if competitors cannot copy it with the same off the shelf syrup. A [World Coffee Portal feature](https://www.worldcoffeeportal.com/feature/own-your-beverage-innovation-with-private-label-syrups/) notes that private label is moving from a procurement decision to part of beverage strategy.

For a chain, a custom base also solves operating problems:

- **One weigh, one drink.** Flavour, sweetness and body come in one powder, so a new barista cannot drift by adding syrup by eye.
- **Same taste in every outlet.** A dry base does not depend on which fruit or sauce the local team bought that week.
- **Simpler stock.** One SKU replaces several syrups, sauces and garnishes for that drink.

## What should go into the brief?

The brief decides how many rounds of samples you need. Write down:

1. **The drink**: hot, iced or blended, and the cup size it is served in.
2. **Flavour direction**: two or three reference flavours, and what the drink must not taste like.
3. **Sweetness**: whether guests can sweeten it themselves, or it must be complete in the cup.
4. **Milk**: dairy, plant milk or both. Test the same dose with each.
5. **With or without coffee**: whether the base carries the flavour and the barista adds espresso, as with a [Raf base](/raf-coffee), or whether it is a coffee free drink.
6. **Volume**: expected cups per outlet per day and number of outlets.
7. **Compliance needs**: Halal, allergen limits, labelling language.

## Can you prototype before a custom base exists?

Yes. Build the drink first from stock bases plus one or two fresh accents, and test it with guests before anyone formulates a new powder.

An example from our own signature collection, the **Salted Caramel Berry** in an 8 oz cup:

- 20 g salted caramel sundae Raf base
- 200 ml milk
- 20 ml espresso
- 20 g cranberry compote

1. Combine the milk, Raf base, espresso and cranberry compote in a steaming pitcher.
2. Steam to 65°C.
3. Serve in an 8 oz cup and garnish with dried cranberries or orange zest.

If a drink like this sells, the next step is a custom base that carries the caramel and berry notes in one powder, so the compote step disappears at the bar.

## How do you test custom base samples?

Test each sample round the same way:

1. Use one fixed dose, one cup size and one milk per round.
2. Weigh every dose on a gram scale.
3. Taste blind against the prototype and against the previous round.
4. Check the hard cases: plant milk, iced service and a drink left to stand for 10 minutes.
5. Write feedback as specific changes ("less sweet", "more berry at the finish") rather than a score.

## Why run a pilot before rollout?

Lab tastings do not show speed at peak hour or how guests reorder. Run the drink in one or two outlets first, and track:

- Cups sold per day against the rest of the category
- Remakes and complaints
- Preparation time at the bar
- Whether baristas follow the dose without supervision

Only then fix the recipe card, train the remaining outlets and set the reorder quantity.

## FAQ

### How is a custom drink base different from a syrup?

A custom base is a dry powder that carries flavour, sweetness and body in one dose. A syrup usually adds flavour and sweetness only, so milk, powders and sauces still have to be balanced at the bar.

### Do Raf and cream latte bases contain coffee?

No. THE BASE Raf and Cream Latte bases contain no coffee, so the barista adds fresh espresso and the chain keeps control of its coffee.

### Can a custom base work with plant milk and iced drinks?

It should be tested for both in the sample stage. THE BASE bases are medium sweet, work with plant milk and can be served iced.

### Should a small chain start with a custom base?

Usually not. Start with stock bases and fresh accents, prove the drink sells, then move to a custom base when volume and consistency justify it.
