A syrup adds flavour and sweetness to a drink that already has its body, such as espresso and milk. A powder premix carries flavour, sweetness and body in one weighed dose, so it suits drinks that have to be built from scratch: chai, hot chocolate, frappe, milkshake, matcha, iced tea and cordial. Most cafés end up using both, with a powder base for the drink and syrup only as a measured accent.

## What is the difference between a powder premix and a syrup?

- **What it carries.** A syrup is mostly sugar, water and flavour. A powder base can also carry milk solids, cocoa, tea or texture, which is why it produces a fuller drink on its own.
- **How it is dosed.** Syrup goes in by pump or millilitre. Powder is weighed in grams.
- **Mouthfeel.** In milk drinks, powders usually give a thicker, creamier result than syrups, as this [comparison of powder, syrup and purée formats](https://bubbleteasuppliers.com/fruit-milk-tea-ingredients-puree-vs-syrup-vs-powder-shop-guide/) notes for milk tea.
- **Storage.** Powder is dry stock. Syrup is a liquid that has to be shipped, stored and kept clean around the pump.

There is [no single winner between wet and dry formats](https://www.cafeculture.com/?p=23392); it depends on the drink.

## Which drinks need a powder base?

Any drink where the base is the drink. These are the doses from our barista standard:

- **[Chai latte](/chai-latte):** 25 g base, 150 ml milk or water, 8 oz cup, steamed or stirred.
- **Hot chocolate:** 35 g base, 200 ml milk, 8 oz cup, steamed.
- **Matcha, hot:** 20 g base, 200 ml milk, 8 oz cup. **Iced:** 30 g base, 230 ml milk, 100 g ice, 12 oz cup, dry shaken.
- **Milkshake:** 30 g base, 200 ml milk, 100 g ice, 12 oz cup, blended 30 seconds.
- **[Frappe](/frappe):** 35 g base, 200 ml milk, 150 g ice, 14 oz cup, blended 30 seconds.
- **Iced tea:** 20 g base, 150 ml water, 150 g ice, 12 oz cup, shaken.
- **Cordial:** 15 g base, 150 ml sparkling water, 150 g ice, 12 oz cup, built in the glass.

Built from syrup, these drinks need extra components to get the body back, and each one is another dose to get wrong.

## Where does syrup still make sense?

Syrup is simpler when the drink already has body and needs only a flavour note:

- A flavoured latte or iced coffee built on espresso and milk.
- A small sweetness adjustment in an approved recipe. Our standard allows **5 g sugar syrup**, and only in recipes that call for it.
- A seasonal accent on top of a base, such as a few millilitres of rose or vanilla.

Keep one rule from our standard: **a maximum of two flavour accents per drink**.

## How do Raf and cream latte bases fit in?

They sit between the two formats. A Raf or cream latte base carries flavour, sweetness and creamy body but **contains no coffee**, so the barista adds fresh espresso. The standard [Raf](/raf-coffee) build is 20 g base, 200 ml milk and 20 ml espresso in an 8 oz cup, steamed.

## How do you compare the cost of powder and syrup?

Compare **cost per cup**, not price per kilogram or per litre.

1. Write the full recipe for the same drink in both formats, including every component the syrup version needs to reach the same body.
2. For each component, divide pack price by pack size and multiply by the dose.
3. Add waste: syrup left in pumps and bottles, powder spilled or overdosed.

## How should a café test both formats?

1. Pick the two or three drinks you sell most and make each in both formats, same cup, milk and barista.
2. Weigh every dose and taste blind, hot, iced and once with plant milk.
3. Leave one cup to stand for 10 minutes and check for settling.
4. Time each build. Speed at peak hour counts as much as taste.

## FAQ

### Do powder bases dissolve in iced drinks?

Yes, if the powder is mixed into the liquid first. Dry shake it with the milk, or blend it, before the drink goes over ice, so nothing settles at the bottom of the cup.

### Is a powder premix sweeter than syrup?

It depends on the product. THE BASE bases are medium sweet, so taste at the standard dose before adding any syrup.

### Can a café use only powder bases and no syrups?

Many drinks work that way, but most cafés keep one or two syrups for flavoured espresso drinks and small accents.
