**Raf coffee** is an espresso drink in which the espresso, the dairy and a sweet vanilla base are steamed together in one pitcher, instead of pouring espresso into separately steamed milk. The result is thicker, sweeter and more dessert-like than a latte. For a café, it is a premium milk drink that sells well to guests who find a latte too plain.

## Where does raf coffee come from?

Raf was created in a Moscow coffee house in the late 1990s for a regular customer named Rafael, and other guests began ordering "the same coffee as for Raf" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raf_coffee)). It became one of the most ordered drinks in Russia and the wider CIS, and it now appears on menus in Dubai and across the GCC, where many guests already know it from travel.

## What is the difference between raf and a latte?

Three things separate them:

- **Steaming method:** in a latte, milk is steamed alone and espresso is poured in. In a raf, espresso, milk and the base go into the pitcher together and are steamed as one.
- **Sweetness:** a raf is sweet by design, with a vanilla or flavoured base built in. A latte is unsweetened unless syrup is added.
- **Texture:** steaming everything together gives a dense, silky body with fine foam, closer to a melted ice cream than a flat white.

## What is the standard dose for a raf?

Our barista standard for a hot raf in an **8 oz cup**:

1. **20 g** RAF base
2. **200 ml** milk
3. **20 ml** espresso
4. Combine all three in a steaming pitcher and steam until smooth, at **60 to 65°C**.
5. Pour into the cup, leaving 10 to 20 ml headroom.

Stay within this temperature range for a stable, silky foam. Keep the base dose fixed so every shift serves the same drink.

## Can raf coffee be served iced?

Yes. Iced raf is usually blended, which keeps the creamy body that guests expect. Below are two iced builds from our signature collection, both in a **12 oz** glass.

### Banana Split RAF

- 30 g Banana Ice Cream Salted Caramel RAF base
- 200 ml milk
- 20 ml espresso
- 15 ml date syrup
- 30 g ripe pear, peeled and chopped
- 100 g ice

Blend the base, cold milk, date syrup and pear. Add the espresso and ice, blend until smooth and creamy, and pour into a 12 oz glass. Garnish with a pear slice, a banana chip or a light dusting of cinnamon. The date syrup makes this a natural fit for Gulf menus.

### Sunrise Citrus RAF

- 30 g Orange Sundae RAF base
- 200 ml milk
- 20 ml espresso
- 40 ml fresh orange juice
- 30 g fresh mango chunks
- 30 g fresh pineapple chunks
- A pinch of orange zest
- 100 g ice

Blend everything with ice until smooth, pour into a 12 oz glass and garnish with an orange slice, mango pieces or mint. This is a strong summer and brunch item.

## Which flavours work best in a raf?

Our flavour pairing card lists **caramel, pistachio, banana, pomegranate, peach and cherry** as the strongest accents for raf. Keep to a maximum of two flavour accents per drink: base, flavour, finish. More than that and the espresso disappears.

## Why use a raf base instead of cream and sugar?

A traditional raf uses cream and vanilla sugar weighed per cup. That works in a small bar, but it is hard to keep consistent across shifts and branches. A dry base gives you:

- One weighed dose instead of three or four ingredients
- The same sweetness and texture in every cup
- Longer shelf life than fresh cream
- Easy flavour rotation without new syrups on the bar

## FAQ

### Does raf coffee contain cream?

The classic version uses cream. Café versions made with a raf base use regular milk or plant milk, because the base supplies the creamy body and sweetness.

### Is raf coffee stronger than a latte?

Not necessarily. Our standard raf uses one 20 ml espresso shot. It tastes softer because the sweet, dense milk rounds off the espresso.

### Can I make raf with oat or almond milk?

Yes. THE BASE RAF bases work with plant milk. Steam to the same 60 to 65°C and keep the 20 g dose.

### How many flavours should a café offer?

Start with three: a vanilla classic, one nut or caramel option and one seasonal fruit option. Rotate the seasonal one every few months.
