To cut drink waste in a café, measure before you prepare: weigh every powder dose, pour milk to a marked line or a scale before steaming, match the pitcher to the cup and prepare toppings in batches sized to the next hour of sales. Then log every remake and every pour down the sink for two weeks, because most bars cannot see their waste until someone writes it down.

## How much does drink waste cost a café?

More than it looks, because it happens in small amounts on every cup. When coffee consultant Scott Rao asked baristas to collect leftover steamed milk for each shift, [one café found it was losing almost USD 15 of milk a day](https://www.scottrao.com/blog/milk-jug), about USD 5,000 a year. A simple check on your own bar:

1. A 60 ml over pour on each milk drink.
2. Multiplied by 200 milk drinks a day.
3. Equals **12 litres of milk** poured away daily.

Waste reduction is also a national target. The UAE's food loss and waste initiative, Ne'ma, aims to [halve food waste by 2030](https://www.bi.team/?p=27647), and operators are starting to measure it.

## Where does waste happen on a drinks bar?

Four places account for most of it:

- **Leftover steamed milk** in oversized pitchers
- **Powder scooped by eye** instead of weighed, so doses drift up
- **Toppings and cold foam** whipped in large batches that collapse before they are used
- **Remakes**: drinks that are wrong, too sweet, overfilled or spilled

## How do you stop milk waste at the steam wand?

Pour only what the drink needs. Our barista standard uses **200 ml milk** for an 8 oz hot chocolate, matcha or [Raf](/raf-coffee), and **150 ml** for an 8 oz chai latte.

1. Mark fill lines inside each pitcher for 150 ml and 200 ml.
2. Use one pitcher size per drink size. Fresh Cup notes that an over pour in a [large pitcher can waste a whole drink of milk](https://freshcup.com/pitcher-primer/).
3. Steam the base and milk together for powder drinks. This dissolves the base fully and removes a separate mixing step.
4. Stop at **60 to 65°C**. Overheated milk tastes flat and gets remade.
5. Leave **10 to 20 ml headroom** in the cup. A full cup spills at the pass.

## How do you stop powder doses drifting?

Weigh them. A chocolate base standard of **35 g** that drifts to 40 g uses about 14% more base per cup. At 100 cups a day, that is 500 g of base gone with no change in the menu price.

Fixed doses from our barista standard:

- **Chai latte**: 25 g base, 150 ml milk or water, 8 oz cup
- **Iced matcha**: 30 g base, 230 ml milk, 100 g ice, 12 oz cup
- **Milkshake**: 30 g base, 200 ml milk, 100 g ice, 12 oz cup
- **[Frappe](/frappe)**: 35 g base, 200 ml milk, 150 g ice, 14 oz cup
- **Iced tea**: 20 g base, 150 ml water, 150 g ice, 12 oz cup

Keep a scale at each base station for the first month, then check scoops against it monthly.

## How do you reduce topping and garnish waste?

Toppings fail when they are made too early or in too large a batch.

1. Prepare cold foam at a fixed ratio: **50 g [topping](/topping) powder with 250 ml milk** (1:5), whipped with a hand frother, mixer or blender.
2. Batch for the next hour of sales, not the whole shift.
3. Portion freeze dried garnish at **1 to 2 g** per drink.
4. Limit each drink to **two flavour accents**. Every extra component is another item to stock and throw away.

## How do you track and reduce remakes?

1. Put a waste sheet next to the bar with three columns: drink, quantity, reason.
2. Record every pour down the sink and every remake for two weeks.
3. Review the top three reasons with the team, then retrain on those drinks only.
4. Repeat the two week log each quarter and after every menu change.

## Does the base format affect waste?

Yes. A dry base is stock you can open as you need it, with one weighed dose per cup. A drink built from several fresh components (sauce, purée, syrup and garnish) has more items that spoil and more steps where a dose goes wrong.

## FAQ

### What is the biggest source of waste on a café bar?

In most cafés it is leftover steamed milk. Marked fill lines and one pitcher size per drink size usually fix most of it.

### Should baristas weigh every drink?

Weigh every powder dose while the team learns. After that, a scoop checked against the scale is acceptable, with a monthly recheck.

### How long should a café track waste?

Two weeks gives enough data to see patterns. Repeat it every quarter and after menu changes.
