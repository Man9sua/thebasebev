Cold foam works on far more than iced coffee. A 50 g layer of foam or cream on an iced tea, hojicha or fruit powder base gives a café a non-coffee signature drink that fits the same 12 oz cup and ice standard as the rest of the cold menu. Below are four builds with exact doses.

## Why are cafés adding cold foam to non-coffee drinks?

Cold drinks keep taking a larger share of café sales. The National Coffee Association reports that [49% of daily coffee cups among 18 to 24 year olds are cold, iced or frozen](https://www.convenience.org/Media/Daily/2026/March/18/3-Stewarts-Shops-Coffee-Cold-Foam_Foodservice), and trend trackers forecast cold foam to [grow a further 25.5% year on year into 2026](https://www.spate.nyc/blog/cold-foam-from-cafe-to-grocery-shelf), with foam-topped teas named as the next step.

For a café in the UAE or GCC:

- **Non-coffee guests** (tea drinkers, families, evening traffic) get a considered signature drink.
- **Colour photographs well.** Blue or pink bases under a pale foam need no extra garnish.
- **One routine.** All builds below share one cup, ice and topping standard.

## What is the basic cold foam drink standard?

All four builds share the same frame:

1. Cup: **12 oz**.
2. Ice: **80 g**.
3. Liquid base: about **170 to 185 ml** in total.
4. Topping: **50 g** cold foam, cloud or light cream.
5. Headroom: leave about **10 ml** so the foam sits on top without spilling.

Dissolve the base fully before it goes over ice, or the foam layer breaks unevenly.

## How do you make an Iced Peach Mango Cream Tea?

The simplest entry point, built on a dry [iced tea base](/iced-tea).

- 20 g peach mango iced tea base
- 170 ml cold water
- 80 g ice
- 50 g light vanilla cream

1. Add the water and tea base to a shaker and shake until fully dissolved.
2. Fill a 12 oz cup with 80 g ice.
3. Pour the tea over the ice, leaving 10 ml headroom.
4. Top with 50 g light vanilla cream.

## How do you make a Blue Vanilla Hojicha Cloud?

Roasted tea with caramel depth, using a hojicha premix and a [Raf base](/raf-coffee), which contains no coffee.

- 10 g hojicha premix
- 15 g salted caramel Raf base
- 25 ml hot water at 70°C
- 160 ml milk
- 80 g ice
- 50 g blue vanilla cloud

1. Whisk the hojicha premix and Raf base with 25 ml water at 70°C until smooth.
2. Fill a 12 oz cup with 80 g ice and pour in 160 ml milk.
3. Pour the hojicha mixture over the top, leaving 10 ml headroom.
4. Top with 50 g blue vanilla cloud and dust lightly with hojicha powder.

## How do you make a Persian Blue Cold Foam?

Deep blue butterfly pea under a golden saffron foam. Floral and aromatic, and a strong year round signature for the Gulf.

- 4 g butterfly pea powder
- 170 ml water
- 80 g ice
- 50 g saffron cold foam

1. Dissolve the butterfly pea powder in the water until smooth.
2. Fill a 12 oz cup with ice and pour in the blue base.
3. Top with saffron cold foam, leaving about 10 ml headroom.

Practical notes from our lab:

- **No lemon or other acid in the base.** Acid turns butterfly pea purple.
- If the drink tastes too neutral, add a little vanilla or light [sugar syrup](/sugar-syrup).
- Other foams that pair well: cream cheese, condensed milk, strawberry, cherry, hibiscus.

## How do you make a Pink Dragon Cloud?

A dessert style drink with a vivid pink base and a slightly tangy topping.

- 4 g dragon fruit powder
- 170 ml water
- 80 g ice
- 50 g cream cheese cold foam

1. Dissolve the dragon fruit powder in the water until smooth.
2. Fill a 12 oz cup with ice and pour in the pink base.
3. Top with 50 g cream cheese cold foam, leaving about 10 ml headroom.

## FAQ

### Can cold foam go on drinks without coffee?

Yes. Iced teas, hojicha, matcha and fruit powder drinks all take a 50 g foam layer well. Keep the base fully dissolved and leave about 10 ml headroom in a 12 oz cup.

### Why did my butterfly pea drink turn purple?

Butterfly pea changes colour with acid. Keep lemon, citrus juice and acidic syrups out of the base if you want it to stay blue.

### Can these drinks be made with plant milk?

Yes. The milk-based build above works with oat or almond milk at the same volume, and the other three use water as the base.
