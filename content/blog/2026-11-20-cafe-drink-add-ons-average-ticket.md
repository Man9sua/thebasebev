The fastest way to increase the average ticket in a café is to sell add-ons on drinks guests already order: a size upgrade, a cold foam topping, a sauce, a fruit purée or a garnish. Each add-on needs a fixed dose, its own price and one sentence the barista says at the till. Track how often guests say yes, and keep only the add-ons that earn their place.

## Why focus on the average ticket instead of more customers?

Footfall is expensive to grow, especially with UAE rents and staff costs. An add-on on an existing order uses the same cup, the same queue and the same barista. [Perfect Daily Grind](https://perfectdailygrind.com/?p=126829) cites data showing that well run upselling can lift the average ticket by around 20%, using small prompts rather than pressure at the counter.

## Which drink add-ons work best in a café?

Choose from add-ons a barista can finish in under 30 seconds at the station:

- **Size upgrade**, from 8 oz to 12 oz, or from 12 oz to 14 oz for blended drinks
- **Cold foam or cream topping** on iced drinks, made from a [topping powder](/topping)
- **Sauce**: chocolate, caramel or white chocolate
- **Fruit purée** in milkshakes, frappes and iced teas
- **Freeze dried garnish** on foam, from the [garnish range](/garnish)
- **Tapioca pearls** in iced milk teas
- **Extra espresso shot** in Raf and latte style drinks
- **Plant milk** instead of dairy

Offer five or six at most. A longer list slows the till and confuses new staff.

## What dose should each add-on use?

Without a fixed dose there is no fixed cost. Doses from our barista standard:

1. **Cold foam topping**: 50 g topping powder with 250 ml milk (ratio 1:5), whipped; 50 to 60 g of foam on the crown of the drink.
2. **Chocolate or caramel sauce**: 15 to 20 g, around the glass or on top. White chocolate sauce goes into the pitcher.
3. **Fruit purée**: 60 to 70 g, in the blender or at the bottom of the glass.
4. **Freeze dried garnish**: 1 to 2 g over the foam.
5. **Tapioca**: 60 g directly into the cup, cooked to the product's SOP.
6. **Sugar syrup**: 5 g, only in approved recipes.

Keep the base dose unchanged when an add-on goes in. A [frappe](/frappe) stays at 35 g base, 200 ml milk and 150 g ice in a 14 oz cup, plus the accent.

## How do you price a drink add-on?

Price every add-on as its own line, never as a free extra.

1. **Cost the add-on**: pack price ÷ pack size × dose. For cold foam, include the milk as well as the powder.
2. **Add any packaging** it needs, such as a dome lid for foam or a wide straw for tapioca.
3. **Set the price** so the add-on hits the same beverage cost target as your drinks, or better.
4. **Show it VAT inclusive** on the board and on delivery apps.

Size upgrades need extra care. Hot matcha uses 20 g base and 200 ml milk in an 8 oz cup, while iced matcha uses 30 g base, 230 ml milk and 100 g ice in a 12 oz cup. An upgrade fee copied from the hot menu can lose money on cold drinks.

## Which pairings should baristas suggest?

Suggest one add-on per order, matched to the base. Our flavour pairing card gives:

- **Chai latte**: vanilla, honey, date, saffron, cardamom, white chocolate
- **Chocolate**: caramel, salted caramel, orange, mint, nut, cherry
- **Matcha**: white chocolate, vanilla, coconut, mango, strawberry, butterfly pea

Keep to **a maximum of two flavour accents per drink**. A third raises cost and usually makes the drink worse.

## How do you know if add-ons are working?

Track **attach rate**: the share of orders that include at least one add-on, a metric bubble tea operators already treat as a core figure, as this [guide on drink shop KPIs](https://bubbleteasuppliers.com/?p=509144) explains.

1. Set up each add-on as a separate modifier in your POS.
2. Pull attach rate and average ticket weekly, by shift and by barista.
3. Remove any add-on with a low attach rate after a month and test a new one.

## FAQ

### How many add-ons should a café menu offer?

Five or six is a practical limit. Each one needs a dose, a price, stock and training, so start small and replace weak performers.

### Should cold foam be included in the drink price or charged separately?

Charge it separately on standard drinks. On a signature drink built around foam, include it in the price and the recipe card.

### Why can a size upgrade lose money?

Larger cold drinks use more base, ice and a bigger cup. Cost each size on its own.
