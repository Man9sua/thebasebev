Since 1 January 2026, the UAE taxes sweetened drinks by sugar content per 100 ml, and the rule covers powders, concentrates and premixes, not only bottled drinks. The tax is paid by the producer or importer when the product enters the market, so a café usually meets it in the price of the powder it buys. Drinks a café prepares and serves in open cups for immediate consumption are excluded from the definition of a sweetened drink.

## What changed in the UAE sugar tax in 2026?

Under Cabinet Decision No. 197 of 2025, the flat 50% excise rate on sweetened drinks was replaced by a tiered volumetric model, as summarised by [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/ae/en/insights/tax-insights/significant-changes-to-uae-excise-tax-rates-for-sweetened-drinks.html). The tax is now a fixed amount per litre of finished drink:

- **High sugar**, 8 g or more of total sugar and sweeteners per 100 ml: **AED 1.09 per litre**
- **Moderate sugar**, 5 g to under 8 g per 100 ml: **AED 0.79 per litre**
- **Low sugar**, under 5 g per 100 ml: **AED 0 per litre**
- **Only artificial sweeteners**: AED 0 per litre

Carbonated drinks are no longer a separate category, and energy drinks stay at 100%.

## Does the sugar tax apply to drink powders and premixes?

Yes. The [Federal Tax Authority](https://tax.gov.ae/en/content/calculating.excise.tax.according.aspx) defines sweetened drinks to include concentrates, powders, gels, extracts and any form that can be turned into a drink, as long as sugar or another sweetener has been added. A dry chai, chocolate, frappe or [iced tea](/iced-tea) base with added sugar is therefore an excise good when it is produced in or imported into the UAE.

Two groups fall outside the tax:

- Products with **no added sugar or sweeteners**, such as 100% fruit juice or a pure tea or vegetable powder
- Products listed as exclusions, including beverages with at least 75% milk or milk substitute, baby food and products for special dietary or medical use

## Do cafés pay excise tax on the drinks they serve?

Not on the drink in the cup. The exclusions in the decision cover sweetened drinks prepared in restaurants or similar establishments and served in open, unsealed containers for immediate consumption. A chai latte made at your bar is not itself an excise good.

Registration duties sit with producers, importers and stockpilers. A café that only buys from a UAE supplier normally has no excise registration to make. A café or chain that **imports powders directly** becomes the importer and has to register on EmaraTax before the first shipment. Confirm your position with a tax adviser if you import.

## How is the tax on a powder calculated?

The tier is set by the sugar content of the **finished drink**, not of the powder. The volume of that drink comes from the preparation instructions on the label. Where the manufacturer gives none, the FTA applies a default formula of total sugar in grams × 20 ml, according to KPMG's summary.

A worked example with a 25 g dose, the standard dose for a [chai latte base](/chai-latte):

1. One 1 kg bag gives 40 drinks at 25 g each.
2. If the label says each dose makes 175 ml, the bag makes 7 litres of finished drink.
3. In the high sugar tier, that is 7 × AED 1.09 = **AED 7.63 per kg**, about AED 0.19 per cup.
4. In the moderate tier, it is 7 × AED 0.79 = **AED 5.53 per kg**.

This is an illustration of the method, not the tier of any specific product.

## What happens if a product has no sugar certificate?

It is taxed at the top rate. The [FTA requires](https://tax.gov.ae/en/media.centre/News/federal.tax.authority.underscores.requirement.to.submit.approved.conformity.certificates.to.avoid.classification.of.the.product.as.highsugar.sweetened.drink.aspx) producers, importers and stockpilers to obtain an Emirates Conformity Certificate for sugar and sweetener content and submit it when registering each product. Without it, the product is classed as high sugar until a laboratory report is provided.

## What should café buyers ask a premix supplier?

Add these to your supplier checklist next to Halal and food safety certificates:

1. **Is the product registered with the FTA** as an excise good, and in which tier?
2. **Is the excise tax included** in the quoted price, and is it shown on the invoice?
3. **What finished volume** does the label give per dose? It should match the dose you actually use.
4. **Does the range include low sugar or unsweetened options**, such as pure powders, for menus where sugar matters to guests?
5. **Who is the importer of record** if the product is made abroad?

You can also control sugar at the bar. Weigh bases instead of scooping, and use [sugar syrup](/sugar-syrup) only in recipes that call for it.

## FAQ

### Is milk added at the bar counted in the sugar calculation?

The tier of a powder is based on the finished drink as described in the label's preparation instructions. Drinks a café prepares and serves in open cups are excluded from the tax itself.

### Are unsweetened matcha or ube powders taxed?

A powder with no added sugar or sweeteners is not a sweetened drink under the rules, so it falls outside the sugar tax.

### Does a café need to register for excise tax?

Not if it only buys from UAE suppliers and serves drinks in open cups. Direct importers of sweetened powders must register on EmaraTax before importing.

### Do the sugar tax rules apply in other GCC countries?

The tiered model was agreed at GCC level, but each country sets its own timing and procedures. Check with the tax authority in each market you supply.
