A café should order drink premix by working back from three numbers: grams per cup, cups sold per day and the supplier's lead time. Multiply daily usage by lead time, add a few days of safety stock, and you have the stock level at which to reorder. Then order enough to bring each base back up to par, and no more.

## What is a par level and a reorder point for a café?

Two terms do most of the work:

- **Par level**: the quantity you want on the shelf after a delivery is put away. Your "full" point.
- **Reorder point**: the stock count that triggers the next order, set so the new delivery arrives before you run out.

The standard formula, used across hospitality inventory guides such as [Sage's reorder point explainer](https://www.sage.com/en-us/blog/?p=21700), is:

**Reorder point = average daily usage × lead time in days + safety stock**

For premixes this is easier than for milk, because every drink uses a fixed dose.

Pure powders go much further. Matcha, ube, butterfly pea and hojicha use **3 g for a hot 8 oz drink** and **4 g for an iced 12 oz drink**. A 100 g tin of Ube Premium makes 20 drinks at 5 g per 300 ml.

Toppings follow a ratio rather than a cup dose: **50 g topping to 250 ml milk (1:5)**.

## How do you calculate daily usage?

Use POS data, not memory. Take the last 30 days of sales per drink, divide by 30, then multiply by the dose.

Worked example for a café selling chai lattes:

1. Average sales: 20 chai lattes a day
2. Daily usage: 20 × 25 g = **500 g a day**
3. Lead time: 15 days
4. Lead time demand: 500 g × 15 = **7.5 kg**
5. Safety stock: 5 days × 500 g = **2.5 kg**
6. Reorder point: 7.5 kg + 2.5 kg = **10 kg**

When the shelf count for chai drops to 10 kg, place the order. If your par is 15 kg, you order about 5 kg plus whatever is used before the delivery is booked.

Repeat for every base: eight bases means eight lines.

## How much safety stock should a café keep?

Safety stock covers two risks: a busier week than average and a late delivery. A practical rule:

- **3 to 5 days** of usage for stable, year round bases such as chai, chocolate and iced tea
- **5 to 7 days** for drinks driven by promotions, social posts or weather
- **More** ahead of known demand shifts, for example the move to iced drinks in spring or the evening peak during Ramadan

If your busiest day is far above average, set safety stock as (maximum minus average daily usage) × lead time.

## How often should you count and review par levels?

- **Weekly count** for drink bases, toppings and sauces.
- **Monthly review** of par levels against actual sales, especially after menu changes.
- **Seasonal reset** twice a year in the Gulf. Iced tea, cordial and frappe rise in summer; chai, chocolate and Raf rise in winter.

Store bases sealed and dry, and rotate stock first in, first out.

## What are the common ordering mistakes?

- **Ordering by feel.** Orders swing with how busy the last shift felt.
- **Dose drift.** Over scooping 5 g on a 25 g dose raises usage 20%. Fix the recipe card first; see our [frappe](/frappe) page for a standard build.
- **One par for all outlets.** Each location needs its own numbers.
