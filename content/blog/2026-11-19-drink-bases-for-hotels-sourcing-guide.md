Hotels source drink bases differently from single cafés because one product has to work in several outlets at once: the lobby café, banquet coffee breaks, the pool bar and in room dining. The right approach is to pick a small set of **dry bases with fixed doses**, check certificates once at contract stage, and calculate orders from covers rather than from habit. Below is a practical framework for F&B managers and procurement teams in Dubai and the GCC.

## Why are hotels rethinking how they buy drinks right now?

Volume is coming back. Dubai hotel occupancy reached 66% in August 2026, its highest monthly level since February, with about 869,000 international overnight visitors that month and 6.97 million from January to August ([Gulf News, DET data](https://gulfnews.com/business/tourism/dubai-visitor-numbers-hit-highest-level-since-february-with-869000-arrivals-1.500673641)).

More guests means more banquet breaks, more welcome drinks and more non alcoholic menu items. At the same time, hotel F&B teams are under pressure to treat beverages as a **profit line, not an amenity**, which puts cost per cup and staff time in focus.

## What should a hotel look for in a drink base supplier?

Use the same checklist for every supplier before a trial:

1. **Certificates on file**: Halal, HACCP and ISO 22000 at minimum for most GCC hotel groups. Ask for current copies, not a logo on a brochure.
2. **One dose per drink**: a fixed gram dose per cup, so a banquet attendant and a trained barista get the same result.
3. **Cross outlet use**: bases that work hot, iced and with plant milk, so one SKU covers several menus.
4. **Order flexibility**: low or no minimum order, so a single property can trial without a chain wide commitment.
5. **Lead time you can plan around**: a stated lead time lets you set par levels before peak season.
6. **Custom development**: the option to build a signature drink for the property or brand.

## How much base does a banquet of 100 guests need?

Dry bases make batch planning simple because the dose per cup is fixed. Using standard bar doses, here is what 100 servings require:

| Drink | Dose per cup | Liquid per cup | Base for 100 cups | Drinks per 1 kg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Iced tea, 12 oz | 20 g | 150 ml water + 150 g ice | 2.0 kg | 50 |
| Cordial, 12 oz | 15 g | 150 ml sparkling water + 150 g ice | 1.5 kg | about 66 |
| Chai latte, 8 oz | 25 g | 150 ml milk or water | 2.5 kg | 40 |
| Hot chocolate, 8 oz | 35 g | 200 ml milk | 3.5 kg | about 28 |
| Milkshake, 12 oz | 30 g | 200 ml milk + 100 g ice | 3.0 kg | about 33 |

For iced tea that also means 15 litres of water and 15 kg of ice per 100 cups. Plan ice separately; it is the item banquet teams most often under order.

## Which drinks cover which hotel outlets?

1. **Lobby café**: [Raf](/raf-coffee) at 20 g base with 200 ml milk and a 20 ml espresso shot, [chai latte](/chai-latte) and matcha. The Raf base contains no coffee, so the barista controls espresso quality.
2. **Banquets and coffee breaks**: chai latte and hot chocolate in winter; [iced tea](/iced-tea) and cordial in summer. Both cold drinks are built without blenders, which matters at 300 covers.
3. **Pool bar**: cordial with sparkling water as a mocktail base, frappe at 35 g with 200 ml milk and 150 g ice, blended 30 seconds.
4. **Kids menu and in room dining**: milkshake at 30 g with 200 ml milk and 100 g ice, blended 30 seconds.

## FAQ

### Can one drink base be used in several hotel outlets?

Yes. Bases that work hot and iced and with plant milk let a hotel cover the café, banquets and pool bar with fewer SKUs, which simplifies stock and training.

### How many drinks does 1 kg of drink base make?

It depends on the dose. At standard bar doses, 1 kg makes about 50 iced teas, 40 chai lattes, 33 milkshakes or 28 hot chocolates.

### Which certificates should a hotel ask a beverage premix supplier for?

Ask for current Halal, HACCP and ISO 22000 certificates. Many hotel groups also ask for specification sheets and allergen information during approval.
